# Content Audit: quadriismail.com vs. Brand Profile

Reviewed: Home, About, Case Studies (index + 3 detail pages), Contact, FAQ, Nav/Footer/Sidebar, chat widget, contact form.

## The core finding

The site is built entirely around **one identity**: "Product Designer based in Lagos, B2B focus, open to full-time/contract/advisory work." It's internally consistent on that identity — Navbar, Footer, About, metadata, the chat widget's system prompt, and the FAQ all repeat the same positioning without drift.

It contains **no trace** of the second identity in your profile — Brand Mentor / Monetization Strategist for digital creatives, PAIPS, Value Ladders, the African-creatives audience, or the 50k-earner community mission. No content here would tell a visitor that side exists.

That's not necessarily a problem — a hire-me/hire-us portfolio and a creator-mentorship brand can legitimately be two separate things. But it does mean every recommendation below is scoped to **strengthening the design-portfolio identity that's actually here**, not to injecting PAIPS/mentor-brand content into a site that isn't currently built for it. Worth deciding explicitly which of these you want (see the question at the end).

## What's working

- **Positioning discipline.** "A Product Designer with a focus on B2B" appears verbatim in the Navbar tagline, Footer, and About intro. No competing headlines, no identity drift. This is the "Clarity > Clout" instinct already showing up in the one identity the site does commit to.
- **The Ryno Finance case study is genuinely strong.** Concrete numbers (12min → 2.5min, 3 weeks → 3–4 days, 62% fewer tickets), a real obstacle admitted mid-narrative (the compliance officer rejecting your first dashboard concept), and an honest "Close" section that owns what you'd do differently. This is close to publishable long-form content as-is — it could be repurposed almost directly into a LinkedIn case-study post or newsletter issue.
- **System integrity across the funnel.** The four services (UX Audit, User Segmentation & Insights, UI/UX Design & Prototyping, MVP Product Development) are named identically in Capabilities, the Process tabs, the ContactForm dropdown, and the chat widget's system prompt. Nothing contradicts anything else.
- **Real conversion mechanics.** "2 Project Slots Available" (scarcity), "Book 15 Mins Call" (low-friction ask, wired to Calendly), and the contact form's interest dropdown are doing actual conversion-design work, not just decoration.

## Gaps and inconsistencies

1. **Two of three case studies are empty.** `linqart.md` and `federal-pms.md` have only frontmatter (title, category, one-line summary) — no Problem/Process/Solution/Outcome body at all. Ryno sets a high bar the other two don't back up yet. Since the Domain Strip and About → Skills claim expertise in FinTech, AI-native, and GovTech, right now only the FinTech claim has proof behind it.
2. **The client logo grid doesn't match your real clients.** "Who I Work With" on the About page lists Foxxie, Mogram, Luniatic, Vindex, Luminous, Limbo, Woxa, Scheme — none of which are Ryno, Linqart's client, or the federal-PMS client from your actual case studies. These read like placeholder names left over from the Figma template rather than real logos. Worth confirming — shipping fabricated social proof is a real trust risk, and it directly cuts against "Clarity > Clout."
3. **Footer copyright says "Odama Studio,"** a name that appears nowhere else on the site. If that's a real studio/agency name, it needs context somewhere (About, Footer explainer); if it's leftover template text, it should go — right now it just raises an unanswered question for anyone reading closely.
4. **Footer's Instagram/X links are dead placeholders** (`instagram.com`, `x.com` — not your actual handles). This is the one place the site invites people into your wider audience/content presence, and it currently points nowhere. Given how central content distribution is to your broader brand, this is worth fixing regardless of which direction you take the rest of the site.
5. **The Hero says nothing.** It's an illustrated landscape image plus instructions for using the chat popup — no headline, no value proposition. Every other section on the page is doing positioning work; the first thing a visitor sees is doing none. For a "Clarity > Clout" positioning philosophy, this is the biggest single missed square foot on the site.
6. **Two unreconciled "process" frameworks.** About page's approach is a generic 4-step "Understand → Analyze → Design → Deliver." Home page's Process section uses a *different* 4-step breakdown per service (e.g., UX Audit: Define Objective → Heuristic Evaluation → User Flow Analysis → Behavioral Data Analysis). Neither is named or referenced elsewhere, so neither compounds into ownable IP the way PAIPS does for your content brand — you have the instinct (naming a framework) but it's not being reused or reinforced anywhere else on the site.
7. **FAQ is now recruiter-heavy relative to the rest of the site's intent.** After this session's additions: 1 general question, 6 recruiter questions, 0 client questions. But everything else on the site — Capabilities, Process, WaysToWork, the CTA, the contact form's service dropdown — is built to convert *clients*, not recruiters. Worth prioritizing the client-question set next so the FAQ matches where the rest of the funnel is pointing.
8. **"Advisory & Mentorship" (in Ways to Work) is scoped narrowly** — "portfolio coaching... for junior designers leveling up." If this is meant to be a bridge into your broader mentorship/monetization-strategist work, it currently reads as a much smaller thing (design-portfolio reviews) than what your profile describes (helping creatives build income, not just better case studies).

## Direct answer to "does this reflect my profile"

Only the design-services half of it. The site is honest and consistent about being a UX/product design portfolio for B2B work — it just doesn't currently know about the mentor/monetization-strategist side of your brand at all. Neither state is wrong on its own; they just can't both be true silently. Worth deciding on purpose rather than by omission.
